J. M. Raines

J. M. Raines writes about the modern mind—how it works, how it breaks, and how it can be reclaimed. Trained in cognitive science and steeped in the study of digital behavior, Raines explores what it means to stay present in an economy built on distraction. Their work blends the precision of neuroscience with the urgency of lived experience, cutting through the noise of productivity culture, algorithmic manipulation, and sensory overload.

Before turning to full-time writing, Raines spent years investigating the mechanics of focus and the psychology of persuasion—how attention shapes perception, and how technology hijacks both. That inquiry culminated in The Book On Attention: Focused Realms and Navigating Attention in a Distracted World, a deep exploration of how our tools and environments have rewired human awareness. Across twelve chapters—spanning neuroscience, aesthetics, economics, and mindfulness—Raines dissects the hidden architecture of the attention economy, exposing how cognitive systems are engineered for capture and how individuals can reclaim agency through deliberate design of time, energy, and thought. Rather than offering platitudes about “digital detox,” Raines builds a manifesto for attentional sovereignty—an argument that focus itself is a moral act and that sustained awareness is the last frontier of human freedom.

Raines’s voice is both analytical and empathetic: grounded in data, but unafraid of poetry. They write not as a guru or therapist, but as a fellow traveler seeking signal amid the noise. Whether unpacking the circuitry of focus, the aesthetics of presence, or the economics of distraction, Raines brings rare intellectual clarity to the defining psychological crisis of our time—what’s actually worth paying attention to.

When not writing, Raines can usually be found in coffee shops surrounded by notebooks, open tabs, and ambient music—testing the very boundaries of concentration they study. Their ongoing work continues to probe the intersection of attention, identity, and meaning in a world increasingly designed to erode all three.
